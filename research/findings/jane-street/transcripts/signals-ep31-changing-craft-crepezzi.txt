Active Record, Petrol, and metaprogramming (00:18:05)

Slop and the code review social contract (01:43:33)

Ron

Yep. I think another thing that people worry about, but that I think also there’s real
upside too, is people worry about the AI slop problem of we talked about how code reviews
in some ways harder and sometimes really intellectually harder, sometimes just emotionally
harder. And one of the results you could imagine is just there’s going to be a lot of
mediocre code that isn’t fully informed on what’s important written by LLMs that get

periods of time about your problem space and your standards and what you need to do to
solve problems in this particular domain that you’re in. And then we’re just going to have
over time worse and messier and hard to deal with code bases. But I do think the LLMs are
also a tool that makes change easier and you can use them as a way of improving the
quality of code bases of that refactor that seemed not worth it and too hard.

Well, maybe now you can do it because it’s just much easier to achieve. And even in the
context of code review where someone gave you something to read and you’re like, “Oh, I
would like to make this much better before releasing, but also we should get it done and
maybe I should just make sure it’s reasonable and get it out even though it’s not in an
ideal shape.” But if it’s much easier to take those changes and improvements and turn them
into a reality, then you can end up with higher quality code that comes out of it. So I do
think a lot of what comes out of all this technology depends on what we make out of it.

John

Yeah, and how you respond to it. I mean, if you’re worried about Slop, one way you can

choice. And maybe you don’t want to make that choice everywhere consistently. Maybe some

moved more toward, yes, it is worth building, but maybe in a way that’s making people feel
uncomfortable where they have to raise this question of if you weren’t going to build it
before and you weren’t going to put your time toward it, and now you have an LLM that can
do this thing, should you actually do it?

Should you have the LLM go do this piece of work that you were going to skip? This is also
hard for people making tools sometimes because it’s like, I don’t know, maybe your user

request, they’re coming to you with code. And it feels like a little bit harder socially
to be like, “No, we don’t want it,” when the thing they’re presenting is kind of a
finished product.

Ron

That’s right. There’s a way in which the whole existence of AI agents has kind of broken
the social contract around code review, where it used to be when someone would hand you a
feature, it made more sense for you to help get that thing over the line because they put
in a lot of work, a lot of sweat equity that went into creating that. And now all you need

a system that will make it overall messy and complicated. Even if the code is good, it

And so yeah, figuring out what’s the appropriate kind of pushback. And it’s just like the

Prioritization questions have always been hard, but they now just present themselves in
different ways. And I think it’s just something that we have to learn to adapt to
basically at a cultural level.

John

Yeah, I think that’s right. One framing I’ve found helpful that I’ve heard recently is

comfortable understanding, yes, that is code. It’s useful to look at so that we have the

Ron

That’s right. And the prototypes can be amazing. It can be like someone has an idea of
